# World Publishing

Published worlds are featured in the Written Realms lobby and search results.
Publication begins with a staff review of a world that its builders consider
complete, polished, and ready for players.

## Review Workflow

1. Every world begins unpublished.
2. A world author or builder submits it from the world configuration area and
   includes a description for the reviewer.
3. While the submission is open, the world is under review and cannot be
   submitted a second time.
4. Staff either approves the world or returns a written review with requested
   improvements.
5. Approval publishes the world. After a returned review, another submission
   can be made once the 30-day resubmission period has passed.

## Readiness Checklist

Review is necessarily subjective, but published worlds should demonstrate:

- **Polish.** The world feels intentionally finished rather than like an open
  construction area. Its listing text and artwork are ready for the lobby.
- **Coherence.** Theme, tone, mechanics, and presentation support a deliberate
  experience, even when they change between areas.
- **Introduction.** A new player receives a considered first experience and
  enough direction to understand what they can do next.
- **Completion.** The world provides a clear ending, multiple endings, or an
  understandable repeatable endgame loop.
- **Consistency.** Room, mob, item, and interaction writing follows a stable
  level of detail and style.
- **Original or licensed content.** The world does not use protected settings,
  characters, artwork, or other intellectual property without permission.
- **Playability.** Core paths, doors, combat, quests, rewards, death behavior,
  and reset behavior have been tested as a player would encounter them.

## Writing The Submission Description

Give the reviewer enough context to understand the intended experience. Useful
questions to answer include:

- What is the world about?
- Is it a story, a sandbox, or a mixture of both?
- What audience and play style is it designed for?
- What does it do especially well?
- Where is its ending or endgame loop?
- Are there important setup steps, commands, or mechanics the reviewer should
  know before testing?
- What future development is planned after publication?

Meeting a checklist cannot guarantee approval; worlds differ too much for a
purely mechanical review. The purpose of the process is to curate experiences
that are compelling, stable, and respectful of players' time.
